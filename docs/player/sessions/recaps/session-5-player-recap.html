Session 5 Player Recap

Player Recap Notes from So far from DM refactor.

Back Into the Dark

Morning came the way it always does in Greyvein—early, loud, and smelling of stone dust. Torvi, Kargun, and Dorne met us at the Lantern's Rest with the look of people who had not slept well and did not expect to sleep better today. Faedove talked them into coming. I did not blame them for hesitating. I almost envied it.

The new tunnel was wrong when we reached the end of dwarven work. Round. Smooth. Coated in something that looked like resin and felt like a decision someone else had made a long time ago. Then two murder ticks came past us—fleeing, not hunting. That is not how predators behave. That is how prey behaves.

We slowed down. We listened. For once, most of us moved quietly around the flooded chamber.

Faedove did not.

She splashed through the water like the pool owed her an apology. I bit my tongue until we found the crack leaking into the stone and sealed it with resin from the dead bugs. Faedove smoothed the patch with her bare hand. The stuff set stiff. She spent the rest of that stretch scraping it off with a dead tick's mandible while the rest of us pretended not to laugh.

The Underwater Passage

Water breathing is a gift and a curse. It lets you go where your body was never meant to go, and it convinces you—just for a moment—that you belong there.

The tunnel under the pool was narrow. I got stuck. Pulled free. Pushed through. And then I was alone on the other side with the weight of the mountain above me and the silence of deep water in my ears, and I understood with perfect clarity that whatever lived down here did not care about my courage.

I turned back.

I am not ashamed of that. I had no way of knowing the chamber was empty.

What the Stone Remembered

We could not reach the hidden chamber on our own. So we did something smarter—we put pickaxes in the miners' hands and let dwarven stubbornness do what adventurer pride could not. They broke through to that same ellipsoidal room I popped up in earlier in the dark, lined with stone rings, bent outward like something had pressed against them from above.

Faedove's arcana work said the rings were seals. Old magic. Powerful. Still present, like heat in a banked fire.

My read was worse.

The damage was not age. Something had forced them. From above. Not water. Not time. Pressure.

We surfaced. Reported. Dorrim paid us for the day's contract with the careful neutrality of a man who has just been handed proof he did not want.

And then we went back in.

I know. We had already been paid. We had already done what we were hired to do. We went anyway. We were curious about what had scared those Murder Ticks.

Something Bigger Than Ticks

Above the pool we found a route the ticks had been using—rough stone, broken through, trending upward. We followed it instead of the cleaner path. One tick in the tunnel. Three more in a chamber beyond. We were finishing the last of them when the floor shuddered.

The purple worm did not announce itself. It arrived.

Eight sticks of dynamite frantically tossed. All of them. The explosion brought part of the ceiling down and barely slowed the thing, would have been better had we hit it. It came after us through the tunnels—too large for the passages, eating stone and forward motion in equal measure. We could outrun it if we kept moving.

We almost did not.

The crawlspace nearly ended us. Quadon threw a vial of alchemist fire straight into its mouth — a critical hit in the truest sense—and for one breath I thought we might actually kill it. We did not. We hurt it. We harried it. We ran.

I incapacitated the worm with a lucky sleep spell long enough for everyone to get clear.

Dorrim listened to our report the way a man listens to weather he cannot control — reluctant belief, immediate calculation. He paid danger wages. Said the elders would have to decide if the mine was worth trying to save now.

We left by cart, bound for Sable's forest.

What I Cannot Shake

The seals were not decoration. They failed from above, not below. The ticks were running from something deeper than themselves. And we did not walk away from the worm — we ran away alive, which is not the same thing.

Water does not forgive. Dorrim said that to me once, and I hated how right he was.

The mountain is not just leaking. Something is pressing against it from the other side of old stone and older magic.

Sable may know a piece of this. She has been living at the edge of wrongness longer than anyone we know.

I am holding my shell necklace again tonight. Cold. Familiar. Honest.

Tomorrow we ask questions the mine could not answer.
