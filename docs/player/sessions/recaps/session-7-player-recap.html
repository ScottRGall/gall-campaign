Session 7 Player Recap

Player Recap Notes from Talieria of the Morning Atol (Tallie)

The Wood With Nothing Living In It

We finished listening to Vaelith. I am not certain we heard her.

The Tides That Keep Me Away

She had pointed us at the ring. My ring. And I had to tell my friends what I have not told them in all this time: I cannot go.

My order walks the Eight Tides. I left my shores for the First and did not look back until nightfall; I gave aid freely and spoke neither my name nor my rank; I slept a full turning of the moon under open sky. For the Second I served under one who holds no divine voice—my captain—and took a craft not of my faith into my hands. I still carry the rope. I accepted correction without calling on the tide to defend me. The Third and Fourth are behind me.

I am on the Fifth. The Ebbing Tide. Five full years away from home, building bonds knowing they are not mine to keep, serving without name or title. I have given it four and a half.

Six months. Then I may go home, and not a day before, whatever grief or longing calls.

My mother led me through my own turning. One day I must lead someone through theirs.

What Vaelith Left Us With

She told us again not to go back under Greyvein until we are ready. When we asked who might take the marks off our backs, she said:

The strongest healer I know is Sable, and she sent you here.

And she told us why she is afraid for us.

The last time Greyvein opened that mine, I had sent three brave people in there, and I've unfortunately had to record their deaths. They never came back.

They were more experienced than we are. They simply went down.

We thanked her and we left. We did not show her our backs. None of us offered. She did not ask.

What We Decided

We know almost no one, and the people we do know live a long way from anywhere. If we are going to be any use against what is under that mountain, we need to be stronger, and we need friends with reach.

So: Brakenridge. It is walled, and several times the size of Edgewood. We will look for honest work and someone worth learning from. No more holes in the ground for a while.

We go by way of Sable's. We owe her GoldBell.

Gold on the Trail

I found it a little off the path—six plants, clean, nothing strange about them. I cut eight fruit and put them in my pouch. Nobody ate one. We remember the boar.

The Fire

I created us a little house out of nothing, dry and warm, that no one outside can see into. Faedove cooked venison for us all it was quite delicious, we'll have to pick more up next chance we get.

Nixlet decided the beard one and the tin one were boring and set about fixing it. She asked Quadon what word the rings in his beard spell. He said "Rock." She asked why he checked his boots first, the morning he woke to strangers. "My favourite boots." She asked Severance whose door he is holding shut when he prays.

None of your concern.

Then she took six gold coins out of his pocket. I made her give them to me and handed them back to him as something he had dropped. A journal should be honest even when I was not.

She misses the water. So do I.

Before we slept she told us what a moth had told her:

There's nothing living in the next wood. Moths lie about everything but they don't lie about that.

I tried a prayer against curses on myself before I lay down. If it did anything, I could not feel it.

The Spiders

They were waiting in the morning. Five of them—three as big as the cart, two the size of hounds—with that purple aura to them.

One threw a web over Severance and he tore his way out of it. One bit Faedove. One took hold of my leg and shook. I burned it with every spirit I could call, and gave what healing I had, and it bit me twice more, and I went down.

I came back with Faedove kneeling over me and the taste of a potion in my mouth.

What I am told, and what I saw after: Quadon killed two of the great ones. Faedove's thunder dropped both of the small. And Severance, bleeding through his armour, climbed onto the back of the last one and tried to make it his. It carried him thirty feet up a tree and threw him. He got up. He shouted at Quadon not to kill it.

Not one of them so much as looked at Quadon the whole fight. Three of us came into this world sideways. He did not. I think the spiders can tell.

The last spider was pulled down out of the tree in the end. Severance moved slowly towards the spider and pet it... Finally it calmed down and stopped attacking him. I do not think it likes him. It has simply stopped.

It still has that glow to it.

What I Cannot Shake

The moth was right. Nothing lives in that wood because something ate it, and we have just killed most of what did.

I told my friends that the bonds I build are not mine to keep. I said it like a rule. It did not feel like a rule when Faedove slid through the dirt to reach me.

Six months.

We are walking south with eight gold fruit, a cart, a horse that will not stop shaking, and a spider that has decided not to kill one of us.

We need to have a chat with Severance about that damn spider, it's scaring the horse.

I would like very much to see the sea.
