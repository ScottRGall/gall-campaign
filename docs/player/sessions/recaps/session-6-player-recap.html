Session 6 Player Recap

Player Recap Notes from Talieria of the Morning Atol (Tallie)

Questions the Forest Could Not Answer

We took the cart road to Sable's the way we planned—carrying the recent events of the mine with us whether we spoke of it or not. The seals. The bent rings. The pressure from above. The worm we hurt but did not kill.

Sable met us glad and tired, and I do not think those two feelings have ever been so close together on one person's face.

We asked her about the seals. That was the whole of it, really. We did not pour out every horror from under Greyvein. We asked who would know what the stone rings were for.

She listened. She does not lecture. She said the seals are older than her knowing, and sent us to Vaelith—an elf who listens to stone the way Sable listens to the forest.

Sable looks worse than when we left her. I do not say that lightly. I have watched people carry strain until their hands shake and their smiles come too late. She is carrying something that is not only worry for us.

Night at the Cabin

Goblins came in the dark. We drove them off. Sable was grateful we protected her—and upset that we killed them the way we did. Violently. Without pause.

I understood both things at once. That is becoming a habit in this country.

We promised to bring her GoldBell Vine Fruit if we find it on the road. Small promise. Honest one.

Greyvein Holds Its Breath

We went through Greyvein on the way to Vaelith's hut. The town felt smaller. Emptier. Like everyone was listening through the stone for grinding teeth.

Dorrim had news. The elders had argued and compromised the way councils do when fear outruns pride—they want the purple worm dead, and they want us to do it. No miners this time. The reward is a staff kept by the town healer for when the mine truly breaks people.

I looked at the offer longer than I should admit. A hearth-crook staff. Warm iron. Silver bands. The kind of thing that pulls pain right out of a person.

We told Dorrim the truth: the job is above us for now. Not forever. Not a refusal born of cowardice. A refusal born of simple math—we hurt the worm, we did not end it, and eight sticks of dynamite didn't even scare it.

He heard us. Greyvein will keep holding its breath... for now.

Vaelith the Listener

Her hut sits where the trail stops pretending to be a path. Dry voice. Old eyes. Ink on her fingers like she has been taking dictation from the earth.

We were still talking when the session—when the night—ran out on us. What she gave us landed anyway.

On the seals, she said Greyvein did not have a name when they were made. She does not know who made them. She told us to visit the Crescent Shrine on the ring—not a shelf of answers, but a place that guards a magical font of knowledge old enough to know. Then she wondered, almost lightly, whether we are worthy to hear what it has to say.

On the worm, she was clearer and colder:

Worms are symptoms. Hungry things rise when locks fail.

She was glad we hurt it. She also warned us—it will have learned us. Our smell. Our route. The way we run. If we go back underground, we should assume it may hunt us. Be cautious below Greyvein now.

And when we spoke of killing it for the town, she did not mock us. She only said Greyvein would appreciate the deed—but we will need more than dynamite tossed near it to finish what we started.

What I Cannot Shake

Sable sent us onward because the seals outlive her forest wisdom. Dorrim asked us to solve a civic nightmare we already survived once. Vaelith named a shrine and a font and a question of worthiness—and cut our certainty about the worm into something sharper.

The rings were locks. Something pushed until they bent. The worm is not the disease. It is what rises when the lock fails.

We are not ready to hunt it. We may not be ready for the shrine either.

But we are done pretending the mountain is only a mine.

Water does not forgive. Old stone does not forget. And something under Greyvein has learned of our existance.

We left Vaelith mid-sentence. Next time, we finish listening.
